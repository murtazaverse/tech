01 / Context

The problem

LLM requests have different compute needs. Serving them across heterogeneous GPUs raises questions about how to balance latency requirements, financial cost, and energy consumption.

02 / Approach

How the pieces connect

I am developing an LLM inference placement layer that routes requests across heterogeneous GPU tiers. Output-length prediction and load-aware scheduling guide placement with the goal of reducing cost while meeting latency SLOs.

04 / My work

My engineering contributions

  • Developing an inference placement layer for heterogeneous GPU tiers.
  • Using output-length prediction and load-aware scheduling to guide routing decisions.
  • Evaluating tier-aware placement against single-tier and First-Come, First-Served baselines using vLLM, measuring latency, financial cost, and energy consumption.

05 / Engineering considerations

The decisions around the system

These are the tradeoffs this type of system needs to account for.

  • Lower-cost placement must be balanced against latency SLOs and the uncertainty of output-length estimates.
  • Single-tier and FCFS baselines provide reference points for evaluating tier-aware scheduling across workloads.

06 / Outcome & status

What came out of it

Research in progress. Latency, cost, and energy are evaluation targets; completed performance results are not yet reported.
