# Space photography and artwork

- `saturn.jpg`: **Saturn, Approaching Northern Summer** (PIA21046). Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/Space Science Institute. [NASA source](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/saturn-approaching-northern-summer/).
- `pluto.jpg`: **Pluto in True Color** (PIA19857). Credit: NASA/Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory/Southwest Research Institute. [NASA source](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/pluto-in-true-color/).
- `earth.jpg`: **The Blue Marble**, Apollo 17 photograph AS17-148-22727. Credit: NASA Johnson Space Center. [NASA source](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-blue-marble/).
- `mars.jpg`: **Mars Planet Globe**, a mosaic of Viking Orbiter images. Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. [NASA source](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/mars-planet-globe/).
- `milky-way.jpg`: **The Milky Way panorama** (eso0932a). Credit: **ESO/S. Brunier**. [ESO source](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso0932a/), [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The source image is unmodified; CSS applies cropping, rotation, darkening, and a soft mask for the decorative background. A visible linked credit is also included in every page footer.

The photographs are stored locally at web display sizes. Partial visibility, darkening, blending, and the scrolling presentation are applied in CSS; the downloaded image files are unmodified. The encounter order is decorative, not an astronomical distance scale.

`asteroid.svg`, the earlier unused `milky-way.svg` texture, and the inline rocket, satellite, and UFO vectors are original decorative artwork created for this website. Rockets and UFOs have attached exhaust; satellites do not.
